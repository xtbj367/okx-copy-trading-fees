## What you actually pay, and what a good year costs

The "0% platform fee" claim is accurate as far as it goes: OKX doesn't add a copy-trading commission of its own. It says nothing about the three charges that do apply.

- **Standard trading fees** on every copied order, at your normal tier rate, exactly as if you'd placed the trade yourself.
- **A profit share** to the lead trader, deducted from net profit only. A losing week costs you nothing beyond fees and the market loss.
- **Funding payments** on any perpetual futures position you hold, paid or received depending on which side you're on. Spot copy has no funding.

Run the arithmetic on a good week. You allocate $1,000, the trader returns +50%, you're up $500. A futures lead trader at the top of the range takes up to $150 of that, which is what RonOnCrypto's April 2026 review uses to argue OKX's futures profit share is the highest among major platforms. The same $500 result on spot at 13% costs $65.

Two details change how you should read that number. Settlement runs weekly, Monday 00:00 UTC+8, so the share comes out in batches rather than per trade. And the ratio can move: because a lead trader may revise it up to three times a month, a trader advertising a low share today can be more expensive next month. cryptocopytrading.app scored OKX's cost clarity at 3.8/5, its lowest sub-score, and noted that the profit share is shown per lead trader with the cap not independently confirmed. ## How much do you need to start? Two numbers, two meanings

OKX's futures copy trading limits page (26 August 2026) gives the per-order amount in fixed amount mode as **10 to 100,000 USDT**. That's a floor per order, not a floor per account. You can copy a single trade with 10 USDT of margin.

RonOnCrypto's review lists a **$50 minimum entry** and treats it as the main reason Bybit suits beginners better. That's likely an account-level or practical-allocation figure rather than a per-order one. The two numbers aren't in conflict so much as measuring different things: 10 USDT is what the order form will accept, and $50 is roughly what it takes to follow a trader across several positions with room for a stop, without every order being a rounding error.

Neither is presented as a hard account minimum in the official documentation reviewed here. Check the deposit screen in your app for the number that applies to your region and account tier.

One mechanical detail worth knowing before you size anything: OKX uses Smart Sync, which maps your allocation proportionally onto the trader's positions. If they risk 10% of their balance on a BTC trade, you risk 10% of your copy allocation on the same trade. Your copy account balance is the only money at risk, which keeps the rest of your OKX balance out of the experiment.

## Picking a lead trader: four numbers worth more than ROI

Maximum drawdown, the 90-day and all-time columns next to the 30-day one, the open positions with their leverage, and the trade history showing whether losers get cut or held. cryptocopytrading.app makes the point about time ranges directly: a trader who looks brilliant over seven days and flat over ninety is telling you something. Where copier PnL is displayed, compare it with the trader's own return. A wide gap usually means followers entered late or sized differently.

The supply side explains why filtering matters more than any badge. A trader who hasn't met the 100,000 USDT lead trading asset requirement can still lead with a trading account balance of 500 USDT or above (OKX futures copy trading limits page, 26 August 2026). One 2026 review claims OKX requires a 30% return over 30 days to qualify as a lead trader; the official limits page captured in October 2026 states the 500 USDT / 100,000 USDT rule instead. Treat the 30% claim as unconfirmed and judge the individual trader.

There is a churn guard: lead traders can open a maximum of 500 trades a day, after which their new positions stop being copied. That limits the worst fee-burning behaviour but doesn't tell you whether the strategy is any good.

## The limits that aren't on the profile page

**Spread protection skips trades.** When your fill price would be more than 0.5% away from the lead trader's entry, the trade isn't copied at all. OKX's FAQ is explicit: "When the copy trader's opening price exceeds 0.5% of the lead trader's opening price, the trade won't be copied." In fast markets you can end up holding a partial mirror of the trader's book, or none of a position they're excited about.

**Copy slots are capped by mode.** In Open/Close position mode you can copy up to 10 trades at once. In Buy/Sell position mode you can follow 1 trader. Choose the mode before you shortlist traders, not after.

**Fourteen regions are excluded,** including the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Singapore and Hong Kong (OKX copy trading FAQ, 11 August 2026). Derivatives access varies further by country, and OKX operates through different entities in different markets, so a feature visible in one jurisdiction may not exist in another.

**KYC is mandatory.** There's no anonymous path in.

**Custody sits with OKX.** Copied positions are held on the exchange; you don't hold keys to them. CryptoWatchdog lists this as a standing counterparty risk, separate from market risk.

**Bot strategies are a grey area.** One 2026 review states that grid, DCA and arbitrage bots can't be copied. OKX's own help centre describes a "bot parameter sync" that updates your copied grid strategy when the lead trader changes parameters, which implies bot strategies have been copyable in some form. The two descriptions come from different periods. Check the trader's open positions before allocating.

**Exiting is clean.** Unfollow or Exit from the copy trading dashboard, close active positions, and the balance returns to your main account. No lock-up period, no exit fee.

Skip it if you're in the US, UK, Canada, Singapore, Hong Kong or any of the other excluded regions; if you want to mirror grid or DCA bots; if a 30% cut of futures profit makes the maths unattractive at your position size; or if you're treating copy trading as a savings product. It isn't one.
